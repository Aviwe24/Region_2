What the SmartCode is

The SmartCode is a model form-based code first written by Duany Plater-Zyberk & Company and now kept by the Center for Applied Transect Studies, which offers it free for towns to adapt. It comes out of the New Urbanism: Andrés Duany and Elizabeth Plater-Zyberk co-founded the Congress for the New Urbanism, which promotes the transect and form-based codes.

Conventional zoning separates land uses and controls density. A form-based code controls the physical form of buildings and how they meet the street, and lets uses mix. The SmartCode organises those rules along a rural-to-urban transect, and every town calibrates its own numbers.

Organised by placeSix transect zones, T1 to T6, plus special districts.
Regulates formBuilding disposition, setbacks, height, frontage and parking placement.
Uses tablesOne column per zone, so a rule reads across the transect at a glance.
Calibrated locallyThe template has blanks and defaults; each place sets its own numbers.

Building disposition

Where the building sits on its stand. The SmartCode defines four dispositions; each zone permits some of them. Street at the bottom of each plan.

6

Private frontages

How the building meets the street: the space between the facade and the frontage line, and the elements in it. Sections run from the street on the left to the building on the right; the dashed line is the frontage line.

Zone sheets

A SmartCode gives each zone a sheet that puts building placement, height and parking on one page. Each sheet here uses one of the panel’s Vrededorp stands.

8

Using it on the panel

Write your group’s rules in this format in panel column B, then show that each of your three typologies obeys them.

Your code, in five rules

  1. Zone: which transect zone each of your stands sits in, and why.
  2. Disposition: which of the four your types use.
  3. Setbacks and buildout: front, side and rear, in metres.
  4. Height: storeys min and max, and the ground floor height.
  5. Frontage: which private frontages, and where parking goes.

Questions for the crit

  • Does every type obey the rules your group wrote?
  • Do the rules reach the Nodal Review targets of 80 or 100 du/ha?
  • Where did you break a rule on purpose, and what did it buy?
  • What do your rules keep of 14th Street and Fietas?

A real calibration, for comparison

Fitchburg, Wisconsin calibrated its SmartCode in feet:

  • T4: front setback 6–18 ft (about 1.8–5.5 m), frontage buildout 60% min, lot coverage 70% max, 3 storeys max.
  • T5: front setback 2–12 ft (about 0.6–3.7 m), frontage buildout 80% min, 2 storeys min and 4 max in neighbourhood developments.

Other towns set different numbers; that is what calibration means.
